# Submitting this note to arXiv

You already have an account and a live submission (`https://arxiv.org/submit/8036221/start`).
You are registered for the **Computer Science** archive (`cs`). That is the group. The
subject class (CL, SE, AI, …) is chosen on the form, not at registration.

## Why you did not see endorsement yet

Endorsement is not listed on the Start page. arXiv asks for it **after** you pick a
subject class and continue, and only if your account is not already endorsed for that
domain.

- If the form lets you add files, you are already allowed to submit.
- If it stops and emails you an endorsement link, that is the gate. Then you need a
  person who already publishes in `cs` to click yes. They are not reviewing the idea;
  they are saying you will submit scholarly work.

Do not hunt for an endorser until that email arrives.

## Pick the right class

You mentioned **CS/CL** (Computation and Language). That class is NLP: parsing,
translation, dialogue, linguistic structure. Moderators will likely delay or
reclassify a software-structure paper that lands there.

On **Archive and Subject Class** choose:

| Role | Class | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | `cs.SE` Software Engineering | Authored architecture, repositories, coding agents, verification |
| Secondary (if offered) | `cs.AI` | LLM agents as the audience of the graph |
| Do not use as primary | `cs.CL` | Wrong audience |

Contract-Coding (Yilin et al., Findings of ACL 2026) is adjacent work. Citing it does
not make *this* note a CL paper.

## License

On the form, choose **CC BY 4.0** unless you have a reason not to. It is irrevocable.
You still own copyright. Later journal or conference submission remains possible in
most CS venues; they are used to arXiv preprints.

## What to upload

arXiv wants a scholarly PDF (or LaTeX source). Upload `paper.md` converted to PDF,
or paste it into a docs tool and export PDF.

Keep the title, abstract, and author line matching the metadata fields on the form.

## After submit

Moderators check that it looks like a CS preprint, not a landing page. They can
reclassify, hold, or refuse. That is still not peer review. If it is announced, you
get an `arXiv:` id. You can stop there.
